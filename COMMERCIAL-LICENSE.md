## What counts as commercial use

Any use by or on behalf of a company — as the noncommercial license defines
"your company" — needs a commercial license. In practice that includes:

- installing POSMA on company-owned or company-managed machines,
- using POSMA as part of providing IT services to clients,
- bundling or shipping POSMA with a product or an appliance,
- using POSMA in internal company operations of any kind.

Using POSMA at home, on your own machine, for your own purposes is free —
including if you happen to be employed somewhere. What matters is who the
use is *for*.

## Why not simply "open source"?

POSMA is source-available rather than open source. The full source is
published so anyone can read, audit and modify it — which matters a great
deal for software that asks for administrator rights on your machine — but
companies are expected to pay for what they use commercially. This is the
same arrangement WinRAR popularised: free for the person at home, licensed
for the business.
